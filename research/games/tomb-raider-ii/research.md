# Research: Tomb Raider II (1997)

Research completed on August 5, 2026. This dossier separates the shipped 1997 game from development builds, later ports, The Golden Mask, remasters, and community shorthand.

## Scope

This record covers Tomb Raider II developed by Core Design and originally released for Windows PC and PlayStation in 1997. It includes the 18-stage story campaign, the separate Lara's Home training area, original credits, characters named by the shipped dialogue and credits, artifacts, weapons, enemies, locations, and evidence checks.

The 1998 Macintosh port, 1999 Golden Mask expansion, mobile ports, and Tomb Raider I-III Remastered are related releases rather than part of the original launch record. No images are assigned in this pass.

## Release Date Finding

The earlier content used November 21, 1997 as an unquestioned worldwide date. The surviving evidence is not uniform:

- The official franchise product page gives November 24, 1997.
- A preserved Eidos press release dated November 24 says the PC and PlayStation editions were “now shipping.”
- Multiple later specialist databases use November 21 for North American and European Windows/PlayStation releases.
- Other catalogues use October 31 or November 1, often without a primary publisher schedule.

The database therefore uses November 24 as `Game.releaseDate` because it is supported by both current official cataloguing and a contemporary publisher release. November 21 remains recorded at regional level as `[documented-secondary]`, and a `[source-conflict]` fact explains the discrepancy. This does not claim every shop in every country first sold the game on the same day.

The three secrets in each of levels 1–16 are silver, jade, and gold dragon statuettes. Completing a trio grants items. The base campaign has no all-secrets bonus level.

## Story Evidence

### Dagger and Emperor

The opening depicts an emperor transformed into a dragon through the Dagger of Xian. A warrior monk removes the blade, ending the transformation, and the monks seal it in the Temple of Xian.

The cinematic's spoken material does not name the emperor. The Core Design archive's descriptive transcript calls him Qin Shi Huang, while the current official lore feature calls him the Dragon Emperor. “Qin Shi Huang” is therefore a qualified archival attribution, not a name spoken by the released campaign.

### Fiamma Nera Chain

The current official lore identifies Fiamma Nera as Italian for “Black Flame.” Gianni Bartoli led it before Marco. Gianni acquired the Seraph; monks sank the Maria Doria; Marco inherited the cult and the search.

The shipped transcript names:

- Claudio at the Great Wall;
- Fabio as Bartoli's seaplane co-pilot;
- Eros as the mechanic who knocks Lara unconscious aboard the seaplane;
- Marco Bartoli;
- Brother Chan Barkhang;
- Gianni Bartoli in dialogue and backstory.

Eros is named in the shipped scene transcript and therefore belongs in the character record. The preserved English voice credits do not assign him a distinct performer, so no voice actor should be inferred.

### Correct Artifact Sequence

1. Lara follows Claudio's evidence to Venice.
2. Brother Chan explains that the Seraph opens the catacombs at Barkhang.
3. Lara recovers the Seraph from the Maria Doria.
4. The Seraph opens the Catacombs of the Talion.
5. Lara recovers the Talion after the Ice Palace guardian.
6. The Talion opens the Temple of Xian.
7. Bartoli reaches the Dagger and performs the ritual.
8. Lara must remove the Dagger from his fallen dragon body to stop him reviving.

The previous content incorrectly made the Seraph the direct key to the Dagger chamber and reordered the catacombs. That has been corrected.

## Gameplay Record

The retail arsenal consists of:

1. dual pistols — unlimited ammunition;
2. shotgun;
3. Automatic Pistols;
4. dual Uzis;
5. Harpoon Gun;
6. M16;
7. Grenade Launcher.

The manual and official classic-era mechanics support unlimited pistol ammunition and finite ammunition for the remaining weapons.

New or expanded systems include climbable walls/ladders, flares, shallow water, breakable glass, a motorboat, snowmobiles, additional aerial and underwater turns, and dynamic lighting. The Barkhang monks are friendly combatants against Fiamma Nera unless Lara attacks a monk.

Released Tomb Raider II versions allow manual saves without collectable crystals. Save-crystal strings found in development material do not describe the shipped PlayStation version.

## Retail Content vs Development Evidence

The Core Design development-build analysis preserves several valuable but non-retail items:

- rocket-launcher and ammunition strings;
- save-crystal strings in a PlayStation development record;
- an unused Croft Manor FMV;
- placeholder level names;
- a possible earlier combination of Floating Islands and The Dragon's Lair;
- alternate groupings for Diving Area and the Maria Doria sequence;
- old Tomb Raider puzzle-item strings such as Lead Bar and Scion.

These are marked `[draft-only]`. They must not be presented as features available in a retail playthrough. The final game uses the Grenade Launcher and has no Rocket Launcher.

## Winston and the Freezer

Winston follows Lara in the Croft Manor training level, and players can lure him into the walk-in freezer and close the door. That action is real.

The claim that the designers built a dedicated freezer joke or frozen animation is false. Heather Gibson said she did not expect players to lock him inside and described what she might have added had she anticipated it. The correct evidence status is therefore:

- ability to trap Winston: `[confirmed]`;
- planned scripted freezer gag: `[myth]`.

## Development Findings

The sequel reused and extended Core Design's proprietary 3D technology. The 1997 team interview identifies refined controls and camera behavior, new lighting effects, flares, gun flashes, and Lara's animated ponytail as important improvements. It distinguishes PC high resolution from PlayStation transparency effects.

Heather Gibson described an intense schedule in which vehicle systems were sometimes unfinished when level construction began. She also confirmed that the team deliberately made Barkhang monks retaliate only when attacked and that Floating Islands drew inspiration from Chinese myths and mirage-like islands.

The official 2024 women-of-Core retrospective credits Vicky Arnold with writing and Heather Stevens (née Gibson) as one of the two main level designers. It says she worked on 11 Tomb Raider II levels, three co-designed.

Sony and Eidos announced on September 18, 1997 that PlayStation would be the exclusive console for Tomb Raider. Whatever earlier conversion discussions existed, Tomb Raider II did not receive a commercial Sega Saturn release.

## The Golden Mask

The expansion's level count is easy to report incorrectly:

- four main levels;
- twelve expansion secrets;
- collecting all twelve unlocks Nightmare in Vegas;
- five playable levels in total.

Thus “four levels” is correct only when explicitly referring to the four main levels. “Five-level expansion” is correct when counting all playable stages.

The Golden Mask is a later PC/Macintosh expansion and is not included in the original campaign count, even though modern remastered packages bundle it with Tomb Raider II.

## Sales

A Square Enix corporate presentation from April 2009 charts Tomb Raider II close to seven million units. This supports the often-repeated 6.8 million figure, but the chart is rounded and does not expose a precise audited table.

Safe wording: “A 2009 Square Enix chart places sales close to seven million; 6.8 million is the widely reported historical estimate.”

Unsafe wording: “Exactly 6.8 million audited lifetime sales.”
