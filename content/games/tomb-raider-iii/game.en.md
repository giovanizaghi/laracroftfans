Tomb Raider III: Adventures of Lara Croft is the original 1998 third game developed by Core Design and published by Eidos Interactive for Windows PC and PlayStation. The current official franchise page gives November 23, 1998 as its release date; specialist records commonly separate Europe on November 20 and North America on November 24, so that discrepancy is preserved rather than flattened into false certainty.

The game turns Lara's search for the Infada Stone into five connected adventures. India is the fixed opening, Nevada, the South Pacific, and London may then be completed in any order, and Antarctica is the finale. The database separates 19 story levels, Lara's Home as training, and All Hallows as an unlockable bonus level. It also distinguishes the original game from the 1999 Macintosh port, the 2000 expansion The Lost Artifact, and the 2024 remaster.

## Story

Millions of years ago, a meteorite strikes what is then a warm Antarctic region. Polynesian explorers later discover the crater, settle there, and build the city of Tinnos around its apparent power. They carve four artifacts from the meteorite: the Infada Stone, Element 115, the Ora Dagger, and the Eye of Isis. Exposure begins to cause mutations, and violent storms force the inhabitants to abandon the city.

On August 14, 1834, five sailors serving aboard HMS Beagle — Stephen Barr, Paul Caulfield, Smythe, Henderson, and Jonson — land in Antarctica while collecting specimens for Charles Darwin's expedition. They enter Tinnos and steal the four artifacts. Caulfield dies during the escape; the survivors divide the objects, which are later scattered across the world as the voyage continues. Only Stephen Barr ultimately returns to London.

In 1998, RX-Tech is excavating the impact region when its workers uncover Polynesian remains and Caulfield's grave. Company head Dr. Mark Willard traces the Beagle sailors and sends Tony, Randy, and Rory to India. Lara Croft is independently searching there for the Infada Stone. Tony, already mentally unstable after exposure, claims his colleagues died in a mudslide. Lara later finds Randy and Rory dead inside the temple ruins.

Tony has taken the artifact and embedded it in his chest. Lara pursues him along the Ganges and defeats his levitating, energy-projecting form in the Caves of Kaliya. Willard then approaches her, demonstrates that the object did not originate in India, and explains that three matching artifacts remain. He offers Lara the locations; she undertakes the recovery while he conceals the full effects of the meteorite.

In Nevada, Lara crosses the desert by quad bike but crashes while breaching a military installation. Captured and stripped of her weapons, she escapes the High Security Compound with help from prisoners, infiltrates Area 51, enters a UFO, and retrieves Element 115 from its power system.

In the South Pacific, Lara reaches the settlement recorded by Core Design as Kuru. A wounded Australian commander explains that his aircraft crashed in the jungle and gives her a map across the swamp. At the wreck, surviving soldiers fight dinosaurs while Lara recovers the keys needed to operate the aircraft cannon. She continues through Madubu Gorge and defeats tribal leader Puna, who uses the Ora Dagger to shield himself, throw energy, and summon creatures.

In London, a mercenary named John reveals that he works for cosmetics magnate Sophia Leigh. Lara descends through Aldwych and encounters the Damned, disfigured former SLinc workers led by Bob. Bob says Sophia used them as test subjects in experiments pursuing beauty and immortality. In exchange for embalming fluid from the Natural History Museum, he helps Lara reach SLinc. Sophia refuses to surrender the Eye of Isis, and Lara defeats her by using the rooftop electrical system against the artifact's shield.

With all four objects recovered, Lara travels to Antarctica. Her helicopter crashes before reaching RX-Tech, and the route through the base and mines reveals employees transformed by meteorite exposure. When Lara meets Willard, he describes the crater as a laboratory for accelerated evolution, steals the artifacts, and descends toward Tinnos.

Willard installs the four objects around the meteorite pit and dives into it, emerging as a giant spider-like mutant. Lara must remove the artifacts from their pedestals to break his protection and kill him. She escapes the cavern, takes an RX-Tech helicopter, destroys her pursuers, and leaves Antarctica with the experiment ended.

## Gameplay

Tomb Raider III retains the classic grid-based third-person movement, manual jumps, ledge grabs, swimming, switches, movable blocks, traps, and automatic combat targeting. The revised geometry supports triangular surfaces and more organic-looking terrain, while many stages contain alternate or intersecting routes. The released game is not completely non-linear at level scale, but it is less rigid than its predecessors.

Lara gains sprinting with a stamina meter, crouching, crawling through low passages, and hand-over-hand movement beneath climbable ceilings. She can also monkey swing, use ziplines, climb selected walls, wade, and interact with a broader set of environmental hazards. Poison persists after snake or dart attacks until a medipack is used. Cold water in Antarctica introduces an exposure meter that drains health after Lara leaves the water if she cannot warm up quickly.

Five vehicle types are integrated into the levels: quad bike, kayak, underwater propulsion unit, inflatable motor boat, and mine cart. Their handling and hazards are specific to the environment — from Ganges ravines and Nevada fences to Madubu rapids, London's flooded passages, and Antarctic rail switches.

The complete retail arsenal has eight weapons: dual pistols, shotgun, Desert Eagle, dual Uzis, MP5, rocket launcher, grenade launcher, and harpoon gun. The pistols use unlimited ammunition; all other weapons consume pickups. The Desert Eagle and rocket launcher are new to this game, while the MP5 replaces Tomb Raider II's M16.

Saving differs by platform. The original PlayStation edition consumes crystals collected during play. Windows and the later Macintosh port allow unrestricted manual saves; crystals still appear there but restore a small amount of health. This difference matters when comparing the original difficulty across platforms.

The classic secret counter expects 59 secrets to unlock All Hallows. There are nevertheless 60 reachable secret triggers: Coastal Village reports three, but a fourth can be collected by combining its alternate paths. The remaster regularizes the total as 60. This archive stores the original campaign count of 59 and explains the extra trigger at level and fact level.

## Characters

Lara Croft is the sole playable protagonist. Dr. Mark Willard first presents himself as an employer and source of information, then reveals himself as the principal antagonist. Tony, Puna, and Sophia Leigh are the artifact-powered regional bosses. Bob is a victim of Sophia rather than her ally, and John is the named mercenary who exposes her role in London.

The database also records characters who are easily conflated or omitted: Randy and Rory are Tony's dead colleagues; Billy appears during the RX-Tech excavation; the wounded Australian commander is unnamed and is not Commander Bishop; Bishop and Lieutenant Tuckerman are dead officers identified by their keys; and Winston appears in Lara's Home.

The 1834 history names Stephen Barr, Paul Caulfield, Smythe, Henderson, and Jonson. Charles Darwin is referenced because the Beagle crew collected specimens for his expedition, but he does not appear on screen. The official retrospective says Barr was the only sailor to return to London and later sold the Eye of Isis.

## Locations

The optional Croft Manor tutorial is set near Wimbledon in Surrey. It includes the assault course, firing range, quad-bike track, aquarium, and trophy room. Winston follows Lara and dons protective gear on the range.

The fixed opening covers the jungles and temple ruins of Uttar Pradesh, the River Ganges, and the Caves of Kaliya. The selectable middle chapters contrast Nevada's Groom Lake, prison, and Area 51; a South Pacific island with coastal settlement, crash site, dinosaurs, and gorge; and a modern London route through St Paul's, Aldwych, sewers, the Natural History Museum, and SLinc.

The final region moves through Antarctic ice, an abandoned ship, the RX-Tech base, mines, and laboratories before descending to Tinnos and the meteorite cavern. Core Design's level archive calls the South Pacific settlement Kuru, while a current official lore article uses Kuro; the database retains Kuru and marks the spelling conflict.

## Levels

The database stores 21 playable entries in three explicit categories: one training area, 19 story levels, and one bonus level. The conventional display order is Lara's Home; Jungle; Temple Ruins; The River Ganges; Caves of Kaliya; Nevada Desert; High Security Compound; Area 51; Coastal Village; Crash Site; Madubu Gorge; Temple of Puna; Thames Wharf; Aldwych; Lud's Gate; City; Antarctica; RX-Tech Mines; Lost City of Tinnos; Meteorite Cavern; and All Hallows.

That list does not imply a mandatory Nevada–South Pacific–London order. Once India ends, the player chooses those three regions from the globe. Each selected region must be finished before choosing another. Antarctica becomes available only after the three middle expeditions and all four artifacts are complete.

All Hallows is not level 20 of the story. It is a separate London bonus played after Meteorite Cavern when the original game's secret requirement has been met. It has no secrets of its own.

## Development

The original manual credits Richard Flower as PC programmer; Chris Coupe and Martin Gibbins as programmers; Tom Scutt for AI programming; Jurjen Katsman for additional programming; Pete Duncan, Jamie Morton, Richard Morton, and Andy Sandham as level designers; and Phil Chapman, Jer O'Carroll, and Darren Wakeman as animators. Troy Horton produced the game, Jeremy H. Smith and Adrian Smith were executive producers, and Vicky Arnold wrote the script.

In a 1998 programming interview, the team said work on a new game began in October 1997, became Tomb Raider III in December, and took about eleven months. Development was led on PlayStation: levels were authored on PC, downloaded to the console, and evaluated there. The Windows version was described as a visually improved conversion when accelerated by a graphics card.

Core expanded its existing technology rather than replacing it. Triangular geometry let designers soften the earlier rectangular grid, while colored lighting, transparent and reflective water, ripples, weather, mist, smoke, footprints, surface sounds, and more detailed explosions increased atmosphere. Tom Scutt's AI work added patrols, guards, ambushes, evasive behavior, and interactions such as prisoners fighting guards and soldiers fighting raptors.

Pre-release interviews promoted at least two routes through each level, but Andy Sandham later said many optional routes were removed under time pressure. The shipped game still contains meaningful alternatives — especially River Ganges and Coastal Village — without fulfilling every early promise. Peru also appears in beta globe material, but it is not a released campaign region.

All Hallows began as part of the London sequence after Thames Wharf. Sandham recalled building it for the interior of St Paul's, but memory limits and extreme difficulty prevented it from fitting the main route. Core retained it as the game's secret reward.

Nathan McCree composed the music, Martin Iveson created sound effects, and Matthew Kemp and Peter Connelly supplied additional sound effects. The preserved English credit screen assigns Judith Gibbins to Lara and Sophia, Simon Greenall to Bob, and Nathan McCree to Winston; other male voice roles should not be assigned without equally clear credit evidence.

## Legacy

Tomb Raider III pushed the classic engine to its most technically elaborate form at that point and made the series' world tour partly player-directed. Its dense geometry, darkness, platform-specific save rules, lethal traps, and obscure routes also gave it a reputation as one of the most demanding classic entries. That difficulty is real; the claim that it was chiefly engineered to sell strategy guides is unsupported by the available developer accounts.

A 2009 Square Enix corporate chart places sales at about 5.9 million units. Because the chart is graphical and rounded, that number is treated as an informed historical estimate rather than an exact audited lifetime total.

The Lost Artifact followed in 2000 as a separate six-level Windows and Macintosh expansion. It introduces the Hand of Rathmore as a fifth meteorite artifact and returns Sophia Leigh, so neither element is silently inserted into the 1998 campaign. Tomb Raider I-III Remastered included both Tomb Raider III and The Lost Artifact in 2024 and formalized the secret total as 60.

## Fact Check

Confirmed records include the 1998 Windows/PlayStation scope, four base artifacts, 19-level campaign, five vehicle types, eight weapons, selectable order for the three middle regions, PlayStation-only consumption of save crystals, and All Hallows as a separate unlockable level.

Qualified or conflicting records include the November 20/23/24 release dates, the 59-counter versus 60 reachable secrets, the Element 115/Ora Dagger label swap in some versions, Kuru versus Kuro, the wording of the ESRB violence descriptor, and the rounded 5.9-million sales figure.

False or out-of-scope claims include Peru as a retail region, a completely rewritten engine, the wounded commander being Bishop, the Hand of Rathmore appearing in the base quest, or the 1998 City scene explicitly proving Sophia survives. The last two belong to The Lost Artifact's later continuity.

## Trivia

- Core Design explained that the subtitle Adventures of Lara Croft reflected the game's five separate adventures.
- The Area 51 UFO is larger inside than outside; Andy Sandham confirmed it as a nod to Doctor Who's TARDIS.
- Coastal Village can produce a 4/3 secret result in the classic game by combining alternate routes.
- The original PlayStation crystals save the game, while the same objects act as health pickups on PC and Macintosh.
- Prisoners can fight military police, and Australian soldiers can fight dinosaurs, reflecting the expanded AI interactions.
- The Antarctica levels introduce a cold-water exposure meter rather than treating temperature as a permanent regional drain.
- All Hallows was intended to follow Thames Wharf but survived as the post-campaign all-secrets reward.
- Some releases swap the inventory labels of Element 115 and the Ora Dagger; current official lore fixes their story locations as Nevada and the South Pacific respectively.
- The Hand of Rathmore is not a hidden fifth collectible in the 1998 game; it belongs to The Lost Artifact.
- The original credits list Nathan McCree for music and Peter Connelly only under additional sound effects.
